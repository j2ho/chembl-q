ChEMBL‑Q

A leakage-resistant virtual screening benchmark, curated from ChEMBL 36

Most released scoring models are trained on PDBbind and BioLiP, and ChEMBL-Q is built so they can be benchmarked without retraining. That only means something if the test set is genuinely unseen, so every pocket here was measured against all 38,825 pockets in those two sets — structurally, not just by sequence — and 1,072 targets were held out of the test set on the evidence. the problem

Sequence separation is not the same as a model not having seen it

The usual way to hold out a test set is to cluster sequences and keep the clusters apart. That is necessary and it is not sufficient. Two proteins can share a binding site while sitting far apart in sequence space, and a model trained on structures has learned the site, not the alignment.

Inside ChEMBL alone the two signals barely overlap. Of the 4,540 target pairs whose pockets superpose within 2 Å, only 561 are also caught at 0.4 sequence identity. The other 3,979 are invisible to a sequence-based split.

So ChEMBL-Q measures the pocket directly. Every one of its 1,413 pockets is superposed against all 38,825 pockets extracted from PDBbind and BioLiP — 54.6 million comparisons — by order-free Hungarian matching rather than a sequence-ordered alignment, because cross-fold similarity is exactly the case a sequence-ordered method cannot see.

the bug this found

Our own sequence rule was broken, and the pocket measurement is what exposed it. We blocked a target from the test set when an external hit covered 80% of the ChEMBL query. But what gets crystallised is a domain, while a ChEMBL target is a whole UniProt entry. IGF2R is 2,491 residues and its PDBbind entry is a 182-residue construct, so the alignment covered 7% of the query and the target sailed into the test set with its own structure sitting in PDBbind.

120 test targets had their exact representative PDB entry in BioLiP or PDBbind at pocket RMSD 0.000. Coverage is now measured over the external sequence, where those same hits cover a median of 1.00.

the measurement

What a corrected split actually looks like

For every target, the distance to its closest PDBbind or BioLiP pocket. Train targets pile up against zero — the model has seen them, which is what train is for. Test targets sit out near two ångströms. Nothing here was filtered on pocket distance; the separation comes from sequence blocking alone, and this measurement is how we know it worked.

positive control

A measurement like this is worth nothing without a control. 305 targets have, as their closest external pocket, the very same PDB entry they were built from. All 305 superpose at RMSD ≤ 0.163 Å, median exactly 0.000. We evaluated FoldDisco as a faster route and rejected it on this same test: 79% of its self-comparisons failed to return zero. The exhaustive sweep runs in 72 minutes on 32 cores, so no shortcut was needed.

the residue

What is left is almost never the same protein

1,072 targets were held out of the test set: 978 for direct sequence homology to PDBbind or BioLiP, and 680 because they were built from a PDB entry those sets already hold. What survives that is a different question. Of the 371 test targets with a measurable external match, 166 still sit within 2 Å of some external pocket. Not one of them is the same protein:

Of the 174 cross-protein matches, 117 produce no sequence alignment with the external entry at all, and where one exists the median identity is 0.32; just two reach 0.4. These are conserved sites in families that have diverged — nucleotide and cofactor pockets, ADP, ATP-γ-S, GDP, coenzyme A. Whether a model that has seen one of those has thereby “seen” an unrelated protein carrying the same fold is a real question, and not one a curation pipeline should answer by deleting the evidence.

The remaining five are the honest residual: their own protein is in PDBbind or BioLiP and sequence search did not catch it, mostly because MMseqs2 reported only a short local alignment. Together they carry nine actives. They are flagged in chembl_targets.tsv like everything else.

A 1.0 Å cut removes 13% of the test targets but 22% of the actives, and a 2.0 Å cut removes 41% of the targets and 56% of the actives. The loss always runs ahead of the count, and that is not a coincidence: a fold that has been drugged hard is also one PDBbind and BioLiP hold many structures of, so “pocket already seen” and “has enough compounds to benchmark on” select on the same underlying property.

So the number ships rather than the knife. Every row of chembl_targets.tsv carries ext_pocket_rmsd and the entry it matched. Score a model across the whole test set, or restrict to a pocket-novel subset at whatever threshold your claim needs, without rebuilding anything.

known limitation

The test set is small, and half its targets carry only a couple of actives. That is the honest cost of holding out everything PDBbind and BioLiP have seen: 1,072 targets are held out of it, and the well-studied ones are exactly the ones those sets cover. 93 test targets have 10 or more actives. Pick a floor that suits your evaluation, and say which floor you picked.

how it is built

Eight stages

Each stage is a separate command reading the previous stage's output, so a run can be resumed or a single stage re-run with different settings. The numbering is a real dependency order — the pocket leakage check writes a table the split reads.

pipeline
#commanddoestime
1curatePulls actives, measured inactives and conflicts out of ChEMBL. Labels by relation, not by threshold alone: a censored “>” record is non-binding evidence and its strength is the concentration tested.~30 m
2filter-proteinsFetches structures (mmCIF fallback, shared cache), aligns them, keeps targets with a single pocket, picks one representative ligand by contacting residues.~3 h
3cluster-activesButina clustering per target.~1 m
4build-poolGlobal compound pool with properties and fingerprints.~1 m
5receptor-simSequence identity, then order-free pocket RMSD over 989,121 internal pairs.~6 m
6select-decoysProperty-matched decoys, excluding anything measured against this target or active against a similar receptor.~45 m
7external-pockets
pocket-leakage		Extracts 38,825 PDBbind and BioLiP pockets, then superposes every ChEMBL pocket against all of them.~75 m
8splitClusters at 0.4 identity, blocks any target with an external homologue, separates test from train to a fixed point.~2 m

reproduce it

Run it yourself

Needs Python 3.10+, MMseqs2 on PATH, and the ChEMBL SQLite release. config.json is the scientific contract — running without it falls back to much looser defaults and will not reproduce this dataset. 

# install
git clone https://github.com/j2ho/chembl-q
cd chembl-q
pip install -e .

# the whole pipeline, at the settings this dataset was built with
bash run_full.sh

# or one stage at a time
chembl-curator curate --database chembl_36.db --config config.json --output data/
chembl-curator filter-proteins --curated-dir data/ --n-processes 32 --cache-dir pdb_cache/
chembl-curator cluster-actives --data-dir data/ --dist-thresh 0.3
chembl-curator build-pool --data-dir data/
chembl-curator receptor-sim --data-dir data/ --mode pocket --pocket-method hungarian --pocket-radius 8.0
chembl-curator select-decoys --data-dir data/ --pocket-rmsd-thresh 2.0 --min-matched-residues 15
chembl-curator pocket-leakage --data-dir data/ --cache external_pockets.npz
chembl-curator split --data-dir data/ --seqid 0.4 --valid-frac 1.0
stage 7 without the raw sets

Extracting external pockets needs PDBbind and BioLiP structures on disk, which most people will not have. The repository ships external_pockets.npz — all 38,825 pockets, 17 MB — so pocket-leakage runs without them. Skip both and the split simply omits its two extra columns.
